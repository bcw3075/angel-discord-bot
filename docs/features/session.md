One game session from join to leave: speech to transcript, the wake check, answers one at a time, typed and spoken, costs and the cap.

## Decisions
- **She speaks her answer as she writes it** (Rhea, 2026-11-14, d-5: "Yes I'd like to go with option 1"; `speakAsWritten`, default on): each sentence of `say` is turned into voice the moment it is whole and the pieces play in order behind her quick reaction; the written answer is posted when the whole answer exists. The placeholder post and her quick reaction no longer hold up the answering call: all three start together. A very short sentence ("Yes.") stays with the next one so it isn't a clip of its own. (Angel's choices within Rhea's decision.)
- **The cap is asked before she begins an answer, not in the middle of one** (Angel's choice, 2026-11-14): a sentence of voice costs a fraction of a cent, and an answer cut off half way helps nobody. The next question meets the cap as before.
- **An answer that fails after she began** ends with the apology, and the transcript has what she said and the apology. If it failed after she had said all she was going to (only the written part was lost), there is no apology: what she said is the answer, and the post is her spoken words with a line saying the written details were lost.
- Small talk is typed only when none of it could be spoken; if one sentence's voice fails and another plays, it stays spoken only (before 0.1.2 it was all or nothing).
- **Back and forth** (Rhea at the live test, 2026-11-14: "you replied first but not when I replied to you"): for `replyWindowMs` (a minute; 20 s was too short live: Rhea's "What about a buckler" came 58 s after the answer) from when her spoken answer finishes playing, the person she just answered by voice can reply without her name; the line still goes through the check, which decides it was for her, and the echo guard still applies. At most three unnamed replies in a row; "Angel, stop" and the end of the session close the window. Anyone else still has to name her.
- **Small talk isn't posted** (Rhea, 2026-11-14: "you don't need to post the questions and replies to non game related questions"): when the check marks a spoken line as small talk (`chat`), there's no "let me check" phrase, no placeholder and no post, only her spoken reply. Posted after all when it failed, couldn't be spoken, or looked something up (the check called a rules question small talk).
- Only the short spoken answer (`say`) is spoken and goes in the transcript; the channel gets the fuller written one (`details`) (d-1 as changed by Rhea, 2026-11-14).
- A short phrase is spoken at once while she looks something up (`ackPhrase`, default "One sec, let me check."; made once at the start of the session), and a "Looking it up…" placeholder is posted and later replaced with the answer, so the table knows she heard. (Angel's choice.)
- A spending cap per session (`maxSessionDollars`, default $5) and one per day for everything the bot does (`maxDailyDollars`, default $10, kept in `data/spend.json`): past either she says so once, and the cap is checked again before every paid step (listening, the check, each answer, its voice), so nothing more is paid for; a typed question is told so. (Angel's choice, a guard on top of the Console workspace's monthly cap.)
- **Stop means stop** (review M2, 2026-11-13): "Angel, stop" cuts her off, drops questions still waiting their turn (a typed one is told), and an answer still being looked up is typed but not spoken.
- Questions are answered one at a time, in the order asked; the next answer is worked out while the previous one is still being spoken.

## How it works
- Each check's verdict is logged (whose line, a reply or named, and the verdict; never the words), and the opening of a reply window.
- All outside parts come in as functions (`voice`, `post`, `stt`, `tts`, `gate`, `answer`, `speakerOf`), so `test/session.test.mjs` runs the whole flow with stand-ins and real Opus packets.
- `onSpeech`: decoder per speaker → `packetsToWav` (skips short or quiet) → `stt` → `transcript.add` → `onLine`.
- `onLine`: a stop phrase stops her; a line without her name is only transcript (unless that speaker has a question pending, or a check on their last line is still running, in which case it is kept as the rest of the question); speech that began in the echo window is ignored; then the gate. Lines the speaker added while the check ran are always added to the question, finished or not. "Not complete" keeps the line pending for `followUpMs`. A gate that says stop and also found a question (a "?" in the line) stops her and then answers it.
- Stops go by time: `stop(at)` records when the stop was said, and a question is dropped when a stop was said after the question was (`stoppedSince(at)`), checked when the check comes back and again when its turn comes. So a stop said while the check on a question runs covers that question too. `runAsk` tells a typed question that was dropped, or that the session ended before its turn, so a deferred `/angel ask` never hangs; it checks the cap; then, together: the placeholder post, her quick reaction and `answer` with the last `memoryMinutes` of transcript and `onSay`. `onSay` feeds a `SentenceCutter`; each whole sentence, and what is left when `say` ends, goes through `sayPiece` (the spoken copy, the voice request started at once, then handed to the voice in order through one promise chain, with the stop and session-end checks again when the clip is ready). When the answer is whole: what is left of `say` is spoken (all of it when nothing came piece by piece, or `speakAsWritten` is off), the placeholder is edited to the typed answer, and Angel's line goes in the transcript at the time she began to say it. `lastSaid` (for the echo check) grows as pieces are handed to the voice. The reply window opens when the last piece has finished playing. A "timing for <asker>'s question" line goes in the log: first words written, her answer began aloud, whole answer written (each from when the question was taken up), how long after they stopped talking it was taken up, rounds and lookups. A failure gives a short apology instead of silence, and what its rounds cost is still counted (`answerQuestion` attaches `dollars` to the error).
- Costs: Claude by each call's own usage; listening by billed seconds × (Nova-3 rate + key-term rate, since "Angel" is always sent as a key term); voice by characters. `status()` and `summaryText()` report them.

## Checks
- (se-1) {live} a real session of 30 minutes or more: the phrase, then the full answer spoken; the typed copy replaces the placeholder; `/angel status`'s cost is within about 20% of what the Console and Deepgram show for that time.
- (se-2) {live} with `maxSessionDollars` set very low, she says so once and stops answering; `/angel status` shows today's total.
- (se-4) {live} a spoken rules question on the owner's PC: her answer starts aloud before the written answer appears; the log's timing line gives the seconds; "Angel, stop" mid-answer cuts her off and the rest isn't spoken; a reply without her name after the answer ends still works.
- (se-5) {unit} speaking as written: the first sentences out while the answer is still being written and the post waiting, the order kept when a later piece is ready first, stop while speaking and while a sentence is being made, a failure after she began, `speakAsWritten` off and an answer that came whole, the reply window opening when the last piece has played, the cap only before she begins, small talk typed when it can't be spoken (`test/session.test.mjs`).
- (se-3) {unit} the whole flow with stand-ins: a spoken question, story talk, two-part questions (also when the rest comes while the check runs, finished or not), stop (also while she looks something up, and while the check on a question, finished or not, still runs), a typed question waiting when the session ends, the echo window, skipped noise, the session and daily caps, a failed answer's cost, typed questions, failures, ending, typing only (`test/session.test.mjs`).

## Traps
- `voice.stopSpeaking` only drops what is already queued: a clip handed to the voice after a stop would play. So the stop is checked again when each clip is ready, not only when its sentence was written (the test for it holds the voice request open across the stop).
- Each voice request starts when its sentence is whole, so a later one can be ready first: the hand-over to the voice goes through one chain, in the order written.
- A line said while the check on the speaker's previous line was still running had neither her name nor a pending entry, so it was dropped and the question expired (review M6): hence `checking`.
- Stops were first counted (a question stamped with the stop count when it was queued): a stop said while the check ran came before the stamp, so it didn't cover the question it was meant for (second review). Hence stops by the time things were said.
- A cost worked out as the change in a running total is wrong while two calls overlap (a gate check during an answer); each call returns its own cost instead.
