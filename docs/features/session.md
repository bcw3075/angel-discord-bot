One game session from join to leave: speech to transcript, the wake check, answers one at a time, typed and spoken, costs and the cap.

## Decisions
- **A busy table** (Rhea, 2026-11-15, design d-7; her ask, 10:32: "she cuts in with a quick, I'm ready style responce and then waits for a gap or somone to give some type of go ahead"; every pick the recommended one): when anyone is talking as the first piece of her answer is ready, she waits up to `busyFirstWaitMs` (2 s) for a pause of `busyPauseMs` (0.8 s) and, if one comes, just answers. Still talking: a quick "I'm ready" **in her own words, different each time** (her standing rule, same message: "please try not scriping things like this so it's always the same thing"), then she waits for `busyGapMs` (1.5 s) of quiet or a go-ahead. Her written answer is posted at once either way. After `busyMaxHoldMs` (a minute) with no opening she lets the spoken answer go without cutting in again: the post gets a note, and the asker's reply window opens so "what was it?" works without her name. `busyTable: false` turns it off.
- **No quick reaction over a busy table** (Rhea, d-7: "Skip it"; the others offered were holding it for a gap and saying it anyway), so she cuts in at most once for a question.
- **Something just said, nobody talking** (Rhea, after review 8: "Wait to hear it", the recommended choice; the other offered was "Answer at once"): when something said since the question isn't heard yet as her answer is ready, she waits for it (free, a second or two), then answers, or waits for an invite if it was "hold on". A truly quiet table is unchanged.
- **Lines said while she looks it up go through the same check as lines said while she holds it** (review 12 of 0.1.3; Rhea, 2026-11-15 evening: "Make the bigger change", the recommended choice; the other offered was patching the two holes): twelve reviews had found holes in handing such lines over at the moment her answer was ready, so the watch starts when the question is taken up. Before her answer is ready a "hold on" or a stop counts at once; a go-ahead only takes back a "hold on" (it can't wave her through a table still talking when her answer comes). The same tiny check per line, so about the same cost; at a lively table it is one per line said during the lookup, not at most six.
- **Her "I'm ready" over words not yet heard** (Rhea, 2026-11-15 afternoon, after review 6: "Yes, cut in anyway", the recommended choice; the other offered was "No, wait to be sure"): allowed; her answer itself always waits until everything said is known.
- **What releases her** (Rhea, d-7: "A gap or a go-ahead"; the others offered were only a spoken go-ahead, or only a gap): a gap, or anyone inviting her ("go ahead", "yeah?", her name). Only lines said since the question while she looks it up or holds it get the check (`hold`, wake), a fraction of a cent each.
- **"Hold on" / "not now"** (Rhea, d-7: "Wait for an invite"; the others offered were "same as stop" and "restart the wait"): a gap alone no longer releases her, only a go-ahead does, or the minute runs out; said before the cut-in, she doesn't cut in. **A stop** drops the spoken answer; the written one stays.
- **A new question while she holds an answer** (Rhea, d-7: "Asker: all in one; others: in turn"; the others offered were always in turn, and newest first): from the asker, one fresh answer to all of it, in the held one's place; from anyone else it is a go-ahead: the held answer first, then theirs (which waits for a gap of its own, like any answer).
- **A merged question keeps its place in line** (Rhea, d-7: "Merged keeps its place"; the other offered was the 0.1.2 order, at the back): ahead of another player's question already waiting. Settles review 10's finding 6.
- Angel's choices within d-7: her "I'm ready" is written by one tiny call (the check's model) only when a hold begins, so it is ready at the two-second mark; typed questions get one too. If it can't be written she waits without it, never a stock line. Her last five such lines go with the request so the next is different. The cut-in and (when she gets to say it) the answer are in the transcript; an answer she never said is not.
- **One speaker's lines are taken in the order they were said** (full code check, 2026-11-14): pieces of speech are transcribed side by side, and when the second half of a question came back first it had no name yet and was dropped, while the first half waited 12 s for a rest that had already gone by. Transcription stays side by side; the hand-over to the wake check is in order per speaker.
- "Angel, stop" is acted on as soon as its own words are transcribed, without waiting behind the speaker's earlier piece. "Angel, stop. What does stunned do?" sends the check's wording of the question on (their own words still hold the stop).
- **Her last written answer goes with the next question**: the table can see it and asks about it ("what about that second exception?"), and only her spoken words are in the transcript. Only a checked rules answer is kept: not small talk, not one a lookup failed for.
- A named line dropped by the echo guard (it began while she was talking) is now in the log, so a line she didn't answer can be explained.
- **The echo guard goes by words, not time alone, for a line naming her too** (live round 2026-11-16: "Never mind, Angel" and "Hold on, Angel" said over her quick reaction and her answer were dropped as her own voice, so neither worked, and one had begun before her answer did). A line said while she talks or within `echoGuardMs` after is her echo only if most of its words are hers (`echoOf`, as replies since 2026-11-14); otherwise it goes to the usual check, so a "never mind" or "Angel, stop" said in any order stops her. Her words for that test are her last words plus every quick reaction and "I'm ready" of the last half minute (`ownWords`), and it also runs on a piece joining a line that waits for the rest, so her echo never becomes the rest of someone's question (review 20). What a line naming her that is a question does while she speaks is unchanged: it waits its turn (letting a player cut in is still one of Rhea's open choices).
- **The names of the people heard go to the transcriber** with her key terms (the same live round: a player's Discord name came out as a campaign name from `keyterms.txt`): each piece is sent with the names of everyone who has spoken so far this session (`withNames` in `src/services.mjs`, owned by setup).
- **She speaks her answer as she writes it** (Rhea, 2026-11-14, d-5: "Yes I'd like to go with option 1"; `speakAsWritten`, default on): each sentence of `say` is turned into voice the moment it is whole and the pieces play in order behind her quick reaction; the written answer is posted when the whole answer exists. The placeholder post and her quick reaction no longer hold up the answering call: all three start together. A very short sentence ("Yes.") stays with the next one so it isn't a clip of its own. (Angel's choices within Rhea's decision.)
- **The cap is asked before she begins an answer, not in the middle of one** (Angel's choice, 2026-11-14): a sentence of voice costs a fraction of a cent, and an answer cut off half way helps nobody. The next question meets the cap as before.
- **An answer that fails after she began** ends with the apology, and the transcript has what she said and the apology. If it failed after she had said all she was going to (only the written part was lost), there is no apology: what she said is the answer, and the post is her spoken words with a line saying the written details were lost.
- Small talk is typed only when none of it could be spoken; if one sentence's voice fails and another plays, it stays spoken only (before 0.1.2 it was all or nothing).
- **Back and forth** (Rhea at the live test, 2026-11-14: "you replied first but not when I replied to you"): for `replyWindowMs` (a minute; 20 s was too short live: Rhea's "What about a buckler" came 58 s after the answer) from when her spoken answer finishes playing, the person she just answered by voice can reply without her name; the line still goes through the check, which decides it was for her, and the echo guard still applies. Only small talk counts toward a limit of three small-talk replies without her name since she was last named (a rules follow-up between them doesn't start the count again; Rhea, 2026-11-15, after her fourth reply in a row, a question after three rules follow-ups, went unanswered: "Cap small talk only"): a rules follow-up (not small talk, or small talk that looked something up) never uses it up, nor does an answer that failed or was cut off (she has already asked them to try again). The count is the speaker's own, and her name in any part of a question said in two parts starts it again (review 8). An answer that failed only after all she had to say was spoken still counts, and the limit closes the window even when her voice failed (review 9). When the limit closes the window she says so in that reply, in her own words and differently each time (the answer is told it is the last one, `lastReply`), instead of going quiet; never a fixed line (Rhea, 2026-11-15 10:32: "please try not scripting things like this so it's always the same thing. I'd like her to feel natural in everything she does"; the first build said a stock "Say my name if you need me again.") (Rhea, 2026-11-15: "I also would like her to not just go silent"); "Angel, stop" and the end of the session close the window. Anyone else still has to name her.
- **Small talk isn't posted** (Rhea, 2026-11-14: "you don't need to post the questions and replies to non game related questions"): when the check marks a spoken line as small talk (`chat`), there's no "let me check" phrase, no placeholder and no post, only her spoken reply. Posted after all when it failed, couldn't be spoken, or looked something up (the check called a rules question small talk).
- Only the short spoken answer (`say`) is spoken and goes in the transcript; the channel gets the fuller written one (`details`) (d-1 as changed by Rhea, 2026-11-14).
- A short phrase is spoken at once while she looks something up (`ackPhrase`, default "One sec, let me check."; made once at the start of the session), and a "Looking it up…" placeholder is posted and later replaced with the answer, so the table knows she heard. (Angel's choice.)
- A spending cap per session (`maxSessionDollars`, default $5) and one per day for everything the bot does (`maxDailyDollars`, default $10, kept in `data/spend.json`): past either she says so once, and the cap is checked again before every paid step (listening, the check, each answer, its voice), so nothing more is paid for; a typed question is told so. (Angel's choice, a guard on top of the Console workspace's monthly cap.)
- **Stop means stop** (review M2, 2026-11-13): "Angel, stop" cuts her off, drops questions still waiting their turn (a typed one is told), and an answer still being looked up is typed but not spoken.
- Questions are answered one at a time, in the order asked; the next answer is worked out while the previous one is still being spoken.

## How it works
- **The floor** (`takeFloor`, once per answer, in the voice chain before its first piece plays; `floor.mjs` for the signal). Quiet table: "go" at once, nothing paid. Busy: `this.holding` is set (at most one, since answers are spoken one at a time), the ready line is written and voiced in the background, then (1) a wait for a pause, (2) unless told to hold on or the talk has stopped, the cut-in, spoken and put in the transcript, (3) a wait for `busyGapMs` of quiet counted from the end of the cut-in (Infinity after "hold on"), until the time is up. `poke(reason)` ends the wait: "go", "stop", "merge", "end"; "wait" only changes how she waits. The result: "go", "letgo", "stop", "merge" or "end"; anything but "go" means none of the answer is spoken.
- **The watch** (`startWatch`, when a question is taken up in `runAskInner`, if she can hold at all; `endWatch` when she speaks, lets it go, or the question ends any other way, from `takeFloor`'s finally and `runAsk`'s). `this.watch` is the question being looked up or held (`this.holding` is the same object once her answer is ready, `ready` true). `onLine` sends every line said since that question to `onSince`, by anyone and however it arrives: a piece of a long talk still going on is kept (held, as a long question is, for this watch only) and what is said so far is judged at once, where only a go-ahead, "hold on" or a stop counts; the end of it, the rest of an "Angel?", a released part, and a line said while that speaker's earlier check runs go there whole. The asker's own lines while she looks it up go the usual way first (more to their question is answered all in one, d-6) and to the watch only where the usual way would set them aside; once her answer is held they go to the watch. A line whose usual check began before the watch and comes back during it goes to the watch, unless the check found a whole question for her (then it goes on the usual way; someone else's is a go-ahead) (review 12). Lines said since a question before it was taken up (it waited in line, or they came while its own check ran) are kept 20 s (`saidSince`) and handed to `onSince` when it is taken up, the last six at most; a call waiting for the rest ("Angel, about the grab,") stays waiting, so its rest joins it, and what it says so far is judged then. `onHeldLine` skips her own words coming back through a microphone, asks the `hold` check (told whether she is still looking it up; the plain rule without it), logs the verdict, and acts: stop → the watch's stop (after it ended, `stop(line.at)` with the reply window kept); go → release (before she is ready: only takes back a "hold on"); wait → invite mode; ask marked only the start of a question → kept as a call for its rest; ask from the asker (a voice question) → a merged question set as the held one's `replacement`; ask from anyone else → release, then `ask` theirs in turn. Someone else's line that doesn't name her, isn't a reply she invited and comes from nobody who named her in this watch can make her wait or stop, never become a question of its own (review 10 and 12). A go-ahead makes her go once nothing said is unheard; a "hold on" or stop meanwhile still wins; the hold always ends at its limit (review 9: a wait of 0 ms looped and froze the bot). Talk kept for a watch is dropped when it ends (review 11), so nothing judged in it is judged again. Her words count as said (`lastSaid`) when handed to the voice, not before (review 11). Review 13: a question merged with more from the asker gets a watch that goes on from the first one's (`carry`: a "hold on" still holds, who named her still counts, and a verdict still on its way acts on the new watch through `next`); a watch already stopped catches nothing more; a reply to her last answer (the reply window) while she looks a question up goes the usual way first, as the asker's own lines do; a stop the usual check reads in a line said since the question is acted on, not judged again (someone else's drops only the watched answer). Review 14: a second stop on an answer already dropped does nothing more; a line the usual check declined can make her wait, stop or go, never become a question (unless they said more while it ran); the carry follows a chain of merges. Review 15: a stop the usual check reads belongs to the watch it was said during (`watchOf`: the earliest whose question came before it and that hadn't ended then, from the current one and the last four ended, `pastWatches`), followed through a merge. Review 16: a line the usual check declined that is set aside (`saidSince`, `declined`) keeps that when a watch takes it up.
- `stop(at)` and `end()` poke the watch; a stop said before its question doesn't. A stop the check reads in a line while the hold is live drops only the held answer (review of 0.1.3: the global stop dropped another player's waiting question); a typed question from someone else releases her like a spoken one; "Angel?" from someone else that releases her is kept as pending, so their question said next is theirs. A merge drops the held entry even mid-writing (`mergedAway`, cost still counted) and takes its post away. Her quick reaction and her cut-in coming back through a mic are not checked. While her words are written, a pause or the time limit still counts. A `takeFloor` that throws is logged and she speaks.
- **What was just said is heard before a quiet counts** (review 2 of 0.1.3): every piece of speech is in `hearingNow` from when it arrives until its line has been judged, and no pause or gap releases her while one said after the held question is still there. Every pause or gap is at least `silenceMs` + 0.2 s, since a piece reaches her only after `silenceMs` of silence. After a merge her previous written answer is put back as "the last one", and nothing more of the dropped answer is voiced. The let-go note goes through `typedAnswer`, which makes room for it out of the answer (never the links). A typed question knows its asker: their own typed line doesn't release her.
- **When a quiet counts** (`unheard`, review 4 of 0.1.3, replacing three rounds of patches): only when nothing said since the held question is still unknown: no piece still being heard or judged (a piece stuck 30 s stops counting), and no talk long enough to be words (`minUtteranceMs`, the shortest piece ever transcribed; measured from when it really began, with stretches less than `silenceMs` apart taken together as the piece will be, review 5) whose piece hasn't reached her yet (it comes `silenceMs` after the last sound; waited for up to 1.5 s more; nobody listened to, nothing to wait for). Shorter sounds are never transcribed, so they aren't waited for. A piece is dated by when it reached her, and a line goes to the check while she waits when it ran past the held question (the asker's own earlier lines aside). She goes ahead only when nobody is talking, the quiet has lasted at least `silenceMs` + 0.2 s (so the last piece has reached her, review 7) and nothing is unheard; the end of someone else's long talk whose first 30 s were held as a question goes to the check while she waits (review 7); talk that stopped with something unheard means no cut-in yet, and if the talk starts again before she has cut in she cuts in then, once, when her words are ready (until then a gap still releases her). Her "I'm ready" may land over words not heard yet (Rhea, after review 6: "Yes, cut in anyway"; waiting to be sure meant she never cut in at a table that talks with breaths); her answer never goes over an unheard line. Unbroken talk over 10 s without a pause that ends a piece (clicks, an open mic) stops holding her up; once its piece arrives it is still judged (review 6). After a merge or a stop, `lastSaid` is her reaction and her "I'm ready", or what it was before when she said nothing for it.
- **A turn in line can be taken over**: `runAsk` runs the question, then its `replacement`, then that one's. `ask` merging into a reply not yet begun sets `replacement` instead of joining the back of the line; `withEntry` makes the speaker's open entry for both paths.
- After the voice chain: "letgo" posts (small talk that was only going to be spoken) or edits the post with the note and opens the reply window; "stop", "merge", "end" add nothing more.
- **Said more before she began: one reply to all of it** (Rhea, 2026-11-15, after "Interesting." and then "How hard is it for you to search?" got two replies, the first talking past her question: "Answer all in one"). When the same person's new spoken question comes while her reply to their last one is still being worked out or waiting its turn, and nothing of it has been spoken, that reply is dropped and both are answered as one question (their words joined; small talk only if both were; her quick reaction not said twice; its "Looking it up" post taken away; what the dropped reply cost still counted). Once she has begun speaking it can't be dropped, and the new line gets its own reply after. The next question doesn't wait for the dropped one to finish being written, and nothing it writes after being dropped is spoken. Not merged: once her answer is in hand (even with her voice off, while its post is going out), past the spending cap, after the session ended, or when a stop came after the first line; the merged question keeps the first line's time, so a stop said after it cancels all of it (review 10).
- Each check's verdict is logged (whose line, a reply or named, and the verdict; never the words), and the opening of a reply window. The timing line of a spoken answer of two or more clips gives the silences between them (a long one is a sentence whose voice was not ready yet; a short one is only the join), so a choppy-sounding answer can be explained (Rhea, 2026-11-15); only that answer's own clips are counted, not the next answer's.
- All outside parts come in as functions (`voice`, `post`, `stt`, `tts`, `gate`, `answer`, `speakerOf`), so `test/session.test.mjs` runs the whole flow with stand-ins and real Opus packets.
- `onSpeech`: decoder per speaker → `packetsToWav` (skips short or quiet) → `stt` → `transcript.add` → `onLine`.
- `onLine`: a stop phrase stops her; a line without her name is only transcript (unless that speaker has a question pending, or a check on their last line is still running, in which case it is kept as the rest of the question); speech that began in the echo window is ignored; then the gate. Lines the speaker added while the check ran are always added to the question, finished or not. "Not complete" keeps the line pending for `followUpMs`. A gate that says stop and also found a question (a "?" in the line) stops her and then answers it.
- Stops go by time: `stop(at)` records when the stop was said, and a question is dropped when a stop was said after the question was (`stoppedSince(at)`), checked when the check comes back and again when its turn comes. So a stop said while the check on a question runs covers that question too. `runAsk` tells a typed question that was dropped, or that the session ended before its turn, so a deferred `/angel ask` never hangs; it checks the cap; then, together: the placeholder post, her quick reaction and `answer` with the last `memoryMinutes` of transcript and `onSay`. `onSay` feeds a `SentenceCutter`; each whole sentence, and what is left when `say` ends, goes through `sayPiece` (the spoken copy, the voice request started at once, then handed to the voice in order through one promise chain, with the stop and session-end checks again when the clip is ready). When the answer is whole: what is left of `say` is spoken (all of it when nothing came piece by piece, or `speakAsWritten` is off), the placeholder is edited to the typed answer, and Angel's line goes in the transcript at the time she began to say it. `lastSaid` (for the echo check) grows as pieces are handed to the voice. The reply window opens when the last piece has finished playing. A "timing for <asker>'s question" line goes in the log: first words written, her answer began aloud, whole answer written (each from when the question was taken up), how long after they stopped talking it was taken up, rounds and lookups. A failure gives a short apology instead of silence, and what its rounds cost is still counted (`answerQuestion` attaches `dollars` to the error).
- Costs: Claude by each call's own usage; listening by billed seconds × (Nova-3 rate + key-term rate, since "Angel" is always sent as a key term); voice by characters. `status()` and `summaryText()` report them.

## Traps
- A piece of speech cut at `maxUtteranceSeconds` (`final: false`) is not a finished utterance: the speaker is still talking. Checked as a whole question, a 43-second question was answered from its first 30 seconds while Rhea was still asking it (live 2026-11-14). Such a piece is kept without the check (for `maxUtteranceSeconds` plus `followUpMs`) and joined with what follows. The rest may never become a line (shorter than `minUtteranceMs`, too quiet, nothing transcribed, or the speech ended right on the cut): then the held text is checked and answered as it stands when their final piece brings nothing, or when the hold runs out (`releaseHold`, logged), never dropped. The question's start (`firstAt`) is its first piece's, so a stop said during the first piece still cancels it, and her name in any piece makes it a named question (the check for a named line, the reply count back to 0) (review 7).
- `voice.stopSpeaking` only drops what is already queued: a clip handed to the voice after a stop would play. So the stop is checked again when each clip is ready, not only when its sentence was written (the test for it holds the voice request open across the stop).
- Each voice request starts when its sentence is whole, so a later one can be ready first: the hand-over to the voice goes through one chain, in the order written.
- A line said while the check on the speaker's previous line was still running had neither her name nor a pending entry, so it was dropped and the question expired (review M6): hence `checking`.
- Stops were first counted (a question stamped with the stop count when it was queued): a stop said while the check ran came before the stamp, so it didn't cover the question it was meant for (second review). Hence stops by the time things were said.
- A cost worked out as the change in a running total is wrong while two calls overlap (a gate check during an answer); each call returns its own cost instead.

## Open items
