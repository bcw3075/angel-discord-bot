Answering: Claude with the conversation, the house rules and Archives of Nethys lookups, finishing with what to say and the pages used.

## Decisions
- **A note on the typed answer** (0.1.3, the busy table's "didn't get a gap"): `typedAnswer` takes it and makes room for it out of the answer, cut at a whole character, so the page links stay whole within Discord's 2,000 characters (review 3 of 0.1.3).
- **One less round of looking up** (the full code check Rhea asked for, 2026-11-14: "efficiencies you can gain in processing speeds, without sacrificing quality. Your answers HAVE to be accurate and correct"): measured from the workspace, an AoN search takes about 0.1 s and a page about 0.05 s, so nearly all the wait is Claude's rounds (a search round, a page round, the answer). A search now brings the full text of its top three entries (rules entries are a few hundred to 2,000 characters; over 3,000 is cut and marked `truncated`), so the page round is usually not needed. Lookups asked for in one turn run together. The prompt says both, and still says to look everything up.
- **The newest printing first, old entries marked** (found in the same check, live against AoN): the index listed the Core Rulebook's Retributive Strike ("within 15 feet") above Player Core 2's ("in your champion's aura") because that old entry has no Remaster link. Among entries of the same name and kind, those dated before the Remaster (November 2023) now go after the later ones and are marked `older`; every entry from before the Remaster says `preRemaster` and carries its year. An entry named exactly what was searched goes first ("flanking" gave "3D Flanking" first), but an old entry is never moved up for its name ("attack of opportunity" still gives Reactive Strike first). Two entries since the Remaster with one name keep the index's order: they can be different things (a general feat and a class's own).
- **Their own words are the question** (same check): the answering call quotes what the speaker said, as transcribed; the check model's rewrite goes along as a labelled hint (`meaning`), because a small model's paraphrase can drop or bend a detail. The post shows the rewrite, which reads better.
- **A lookup that fails is tried once more, and a failure is said**: if it fails twice Claude is told to say she couldn't check, the result counts it (`failedLookups`), and the post gets a line saying part of the answer couldn't be checked against Archives of Nethys.
- **Only pages the lookups gave are posted as sources**: a link Claude writes that no search or page returned is dropped.
- **An incomplete answer from the index is an error, not "no such rule"**: a reply the index marks timed out or partly failed is not cached and counts as a failed lookup; a true "nothing found" is believed for ten minutes, not a week. Each request gets 6 s (it was 15 s).
- **Speak as she writes** (Rhea, 2026-11-14, of the speed options: "Yes I'd like to go with option 1"; d-5): every answering round is read as Claude writes it (`claude.stream`), and the answer tool's `say` is passed on piece by piece (`onSay`) so the session can speak her first sentence while `details` and `sources` are still being written. All rounds are streamed, not only the last, because any round can be the one that answers. The answer tool asks for its input as written (`eager_input_streaming: true`, checked against the tool-streaming docs 2026-11-14: without it the API holds each value back until it is whole, so `say` would come in one piece); the prompt and the tool tell her to write `say` first. The lookups' inputs stay checked by the API.
- **A streamed answer Claude refuses is worked out once more the plain way** (2026-11-14, after two live failures): when a round is answered with a 400 before she has said anything, the question starts again with plain calls (`create`), where Claude's replies go back exactly as they came; the log says so (`fellBack`). After she has begun to speak it is not started again (she would say it twice).
- **The tools are the same in every round** (2026-11-14, from the thinking docs: her earlier thinking is accepted back only while what came before it is unchanged; and the cached prompt stays in use). The last round only adds "give your answer now"; a lookup asked for on it is not run, she is told so, and she gets one more round.
- **The last round never forces the answer tool**: (0.1.1; changed above in 0.1.2, it no longer offers only that tool) it offered only that tool and added "give your answer now" (live 2026-11-14: a long Retributive Strike question reached the last round and Sonnet 5.5 refused `tool_choice` type tool with a 400, so she said "couldn't reach my rules brain").
- **Rules come from Archives of Nethys**, through the public search index its own site uses, not Claude's web search: exact, fast, free, and the owner's standing rule is AoN for every rules lookup. Remaster entries win; an old name is mentioned once. (Angel's choice, 2026-11-12.)
- **House rules now** (the owner, 2026-11-12, d-4; the other choices were rules only, or house rules and the party's sheets): `house-rules.md`, player-safe, wins over AoN. The party's sheets and campaign facts are phase 2.
- **Spoken short, written in full with links** (d-1 as changed by Rhea at the live test, 2026-11-14: "perhaps you should be a bit shorter on the voice response? I won't mind a little back and forth"; it was "explain the answer in full" aloud): the `answer` tool gives `say` (one to three short spoken sentences, mentioning anything more so they can ask), `details` (formatted for Discord at Rhea's ask, 2026-11-14, "your text replies are a bit wordy and a wall of text... use formatting": a bold one-line answer, two to five short bullets with the key terms and numbers in bold, an optional *GM's call:* line, under about 600 characters) and `sources`. `say` is spoken and goes in the transcript; the typed copy is `details` (or `say` when there's none) with who asked and the links.
- **The answering prompt is cached for an hour** (`answerCacheTtl: "1h"`, checked against the pricing and caching docs 2026-11-13). Questions at a table are often more than 5 minutes apart, so the 5-minute cache would be written again for most of them. A 1-hour write costs 2× the input price instead of 1.25×, but every question inside the hour reads the prompt at a twentieth of the price and starts the hour again. For a session of about 20 questions, with the prompt about 6,000 tokens: about 30 cents in writes on the 5-minute cache, about 4 cents on the 1-hour one. `"5m"` switches back.
- Angel's voice in the prompt: warm, quick, a little sassy, tells someone kindly when they have a rule wrong, never overrules the GM, says when something is the GM's call, never invents a rule. Everything she says is heard by players, so only rules, house rules and the table's own words are used.
- **She speaks in her own words, never a stock line** (Rhea, 2026-11-15 10:32: "I'd like her to feel natural in everything she does"). When the session tells her a reply is the last one it will take without her name (`lastReply`), the question carries a note asking her to end by saying so, briefly, in her own words and differently each time, and not to mention it if the reply turned into a rules question (`LAST_REPLY_NOTE`).

## How it works
- `answerQuestion`: up to six rounds of tool use (`aon_search`, `aon_get`) and one more to answer; the sixth says "answer now" with the same tools (never a forced `tool_choice`). With a client that can `stream` (the real one), each round is streamed and a `FieldReader("say")` reads the answer tool's unfinished JSON and gives `onSay` each new piece, then `onSay("", { end: true })` once when `say`'s closing quote arrives (so a one-sentence answer, and every answer's last sentence, is spoken before `details` and `sources` are written); only the first answer call of a reply is passed on. An answer cut short (out of tokens, JSON that doesn't parse) still gives what `say` held. A stand-in with only `create` works as before, with no `onSay`. A plain-text reply is taken as the answer. A failed lookup is told to Claude as the tool result. When a round fails, the error carries what the rounds before it cost (`dollars`). Only `https://2e.aonprd.com/` links are kept as sources; with none given, the pages read with `aon_get` are listed. The system prompt is cached (`cache_control` on the system block, which also covers the tools).
- `search` (in `aon.mjs`): `match_phrase` and fuzzy `match` on `name`, `match` on `text`, reprints hidden with `exclude_from_search`, a category filter; then entries with `remaster_id` are swapped for their Remaster version (one `ids` query), with `oldName` only when the category is the same. `get` takes an id (`ids` query) or a page link (`term` on `url`). Results are cached in memory and in `data/aon-cache.json` for 7 days (at most 200 entries, long texts cut before caching and marked `_cut`, so an entry whose spaces shrink it under the limit after the cut is still called cut, the file written a few seconds after the last lookup and at shutdown, not at each one; review L4); a named User-Agent is sent.
- `cost` (in `claude.mjs`): each call's price from its `usage`. Cache writes are split into 5-minute and 1-hour ones (`usage.cache_creation`), priced 1.25× and 2× the input price (a fifth price in the table overrides the 2×); without the split every write counts at the dearer rate, and a model with no price (one she typed in) counts at the dearest known price, so the caps never undercount. `answerQuestion` also returns the cache tokens written and read, which `npm run ask` prints.
- `stream` (in `claude.mjs`): the same call with `stream: true`, read as server-sent events (`readEvents`: events cut anywhere between the pieces that arrive) and built back into the shape `create` gives (text, tool inputs parsed at each block's end, the usage of `message_start` and `message_delta` together). `onDelta` gets each text or tool-input piece. Thinking pieces and their signature are kept whole, and a kind of piece the code doesn't know is kept too (text added on, anything else in a list), so every block can go back as it came. It is sent again on 429, 529, 5xx, a failed connection, or an overload error before anything was written, never after the first content (she may already be speaking it) and never after a timeout. A reply that breaks off throws with `usage` set, and both the client's tally and `answerQuestion`'s `dollars` count it; the count of what she wrote only comes at the end of a reply, so for a broken one it is put at a token per three characters that arrived (the start event's own `output_tokens` is a placeholder of about 1, not the count). `FieldReader` reads one top-level string field out of unfinished JSON in plain character steps (escapes undone, line breaks as spaces).
- `create` (in `claude.mjs`): plain fetch to the Messages API with a 45-second limit; retries 429, 529, 5xx and network failures (twice, honouring `retry-after`), but never a timed-out call (it may have run and been billed; review L8); `cost(model, usage)` from the price table (fresh input, 5-minute cache write, cache read, output).
- `typedAnswer` keeps the message under 2,000 characters and wraps links in `<…>` so Discord shows no previews.

## Traps
- The index ranks a wordy search by shared words: "resistance to all damage" gave three entries about damage dice first and Resistance ninth, and she went through six rounds without seeing the page (live 2026-11-14, 15 to 21 s before her answer). `search` reads thirty hits and puts first the entries whose whole name is among the words searched (the one named earliest leading, old printings never), and an old printing gets no text slot. The little words of a question ("how", "does", "with", "work", ...: `FILLER` in `aon.mjs`) never move a one-word entry of that name up (review 7: "Work" took Persistent Damage's text slot). Longest-name-first was tried and refused: it put Resistance third for "resistance multiple damage types". A lookup that fails is in that log line too. A question that needed three or more lookups writes them in the log (`lookups for <asker>'s question: ...`).
- She gets the rule she looked up right and gets wrong what she adds from memory beside it (first self-test run, 2026-11-14: 26 answers right, three side remarks wrong: a feat that isn't in the entry, a spell that doesn't exist, a condition said the wrong way round). The prompt says to state only what the entries she read say; check a new prompt wording with `Test Angel.cmd` and read the answers against AoN. Read every number too: the 2026-11-15 run was read as right and still had two (a second kick given the full multiple attack penalty though a kick uses the fist's statistics and the fist is agile; a "once per round" limit on Assisted Recovery that the rule doesn't have), found on a second reading on 2026-11-16. Hence the prompt's line on numbers, penalties and limits (0.1.3, Rhea: "General prompt rule").
- The token limit counts her thinking too. A reply cut off at it (`stop_reason` max_tokens) is asked once more with twice the room unless all she says had already come through; a lookup cut mid-way is never run. The bot gives at least 3,000 tokens of room.
- A streamed reply can hold thinking blocks though none were asked for (Sonnet 5.5 decides for itself; live 2026-11-14, the follow-up "What about stacking?" failed with "each thinking block must contain thinking"). `stream` keeps `thinking_delta` and `signature_delta` whole, because the block goes back with the lookups' results. The stand-in replies in the tests now include one.
- An old AoN entry does not always point to its replacement (`remaster_id`): same-name entries from before and after the Remaster can sit side by side, the old one ranked first. Check a ranking change against the real index (`npm run test:aon`): moving exact names up once put the old Attack of Opportunity above Reactive Strike, and only the live check caught it.
- The cache file's write was meant to come a few seconds after the last lookup but came 3 s after the first, in the middle of an answer; it is now put off at each lookup.
- A tool input streamed as written isn't checked by the API: it can be cut short or not parse. `stream` gives `{}` for it (and lists it in `brokenInputs`) and the answer falls back to what the field reader held.
- A streamed call's `usage` comes in two parts: input and cache tokens at `message_start`, output tokens at `message_delta`. Taking only one undercounts the cost.
- The AoN index answers `_mapping` with a permissions error for anonymous users; field types were found by probing: `url` and `category` are keywords (`term` works), the `id` field is not (use an `ids` query on `_id`).
- A legacy entry's `remaster_id` can point to a different kind of entry (an old class feature now inside its class page): hence `oldName` only for the same category.
