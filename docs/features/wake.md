Knowing when someone is talking to Angel: a free text check for her name, then a cheap model decides.

## Decisions
- **While she holds an answer at a busy table, a line has its own check** (0.1.3, Rhea's d-7): `HOLD_SYSTEM` says go (an invitation: "go ahead", "yeah?", her name alone), wait ("hold on", "not now"), stop ("never mind"), ask (a new question, or more to the one she holds) or none (the table among themselves, the game, an angel in the story); an ask is marked not `complete` when the line only starts a question still to come ("Angel, about the grab,"). It is told whose answer she holds and what she said, or that she is still looking it up (from review 12 of 0.1.3 it judges lines from when the question is taken up). Same limits as the check (8 s, one more try); without a reply it can read, the plain rule: a stop phrase; her name with "hold on", "wait", "not now", "one sec" and the like (a wait); her name with little else, or without a "?" (a go-ahead); her name with a question (a question); else nothing for her (review of 0.1.3: "Hold on Angel." had read as a go-ahead and "Angel?" as a question).
- **A stop the transcriber misheard is a stop at once** (0.2.1; agreed 2026-11-22 after 0.2.0's live round, where "Angel, stop" came back as "Angel Angel's top" and "Top Angel."): `isStopRequest` also takes "Angel's top" (her name up to three times) and "Top, Angel" as the whole line, nothing after; "Angel's top pick" or "Top it off, Angel" are not stops. Only the spelling "angel": "Angela's top" and "Top angle" are table talk (initiative, a side to attack from), and a question ("Angel's top?") isn't a stop (review 12). A one-table setup with no `tables/` folder doesn't get the key term (it reads the default key terms); every table of the owner's has its folder. And where she answers, "stop" is sent to the transcriber as a key term (right after her name, so the 100-term limit can't drop it); not at a table where she only listens, where it would only pull "top" towards "stop" in table talk.
- **A stop with her name last is a stop at once too** (short round of 2026-11-21: "Never mind, Angel." said over her answer went to the model check, came back 5 s later, and she had finished by then): `isStopRequest` also takes a stop word, then her name, and nothing else ("stop, Angel", "Okay, that's enough, Angel!"); anything after her name ("Never mind, Angel, how does flanking work?") or between ("never mind the angel") still goes through the check.
- **Her "I'm ready" is written fresh each time** (`READY_SYSTEM`; Rhea's standing rule, 2026-11-15: never a stock line): 2 to 8 words in her voice, fitting the question, never the answer, told her last few so it differs. Written only when a hold begins (6 s, no retry); a reply that is empty or too long is unusable, and then she says nothing rather than a fixed line.
- **The check has its own limits** (full code check, 2026-11-14): 8 seconds (a call that runs out of time is never sent again) and one more try after at most 1.5 s when the server is busy, not an answer's 45 seconds and two (the table is waiting on it); room for 600 tokens and a rewrite of at most 40 words (it wrote the whole question back: 200 tokens cut a long one off, and in the first self-test run 400 still cut off a 900-character one), leaving JSON that couldn't be read; a reply cut off at the limit, or one that can't be read, is said in the log, and the plain rule decides.
- After a reply, a question announced but not yet asked ("Okay. So first question.", "One more thing.") is unfinished, and she waits for it; a question that trails off is still whole (live 2026-11-15: "Oh, that's funny. Okay. So first question." was answered as small talk while Rhea was already asking). The follow-up check could only ever say "whole" before.
- **A follow-up has its own check** (`FOLLOWUP_SYSTEM`, 2026-11-14: even with the note, the general check judged "Okay. What about stacking?" not for her, 19:14): for the person she just answered, it leans to yes and says no only for a line plainly for someone else or a bare acknowledgement.
- **Her quick reaction** comes from the check (`ack`: three to ten words in her voice about this question, different each time), replacing the fixed "One sec, let me check" (Rhea, 2026-11-14: "getting repetitive. I'd love your personality to come through... I'm ok with using more tokens"). Typed questions and failed checks use one of a few varied lines (`ACKS`); `ackPhrase: ""` still turns it off.
- A follow-up that starts with an acknowledgement and then asks something is for her; only a bare "okay"/"thanks" isn't. A reply without her name is sent to the check with a note that she just answered this person (2026-11-14: "Okay. Good. What about stacking with other penalties or bonuses?" was judged not for her).
- The check also says whether a line is small talk (`chat`), and counts a reply to what she just said as said to her even without her name (2026-11-14, for the back and forth Rhea asked for).
- Two steps (Angel's design, 2026-11-12): a line is looked at only when it names her or a word the transcriber makes of her name (Angela, Angie, angle and the like); then Haiku 5.5 reads the last few lines and decides. An angel in the story (a creature, "angelic", a statue) or talk about her doesn't set her off.
- "Angel, stop" (and "be quiet", "that's enough", "never mind" …) acts at once, without the model, but only when her name comes first and at most three words follow the stop word; "Angel, never mind that, how does flanking work?" or "the angel, quiet as death, ..." go through the model check instead (review M3, 2026-11-13). The model check says stop only when they plainly tell her to stop or that they don't want the answer; a remark about being cut off, interrupted or misheard is not one (live 2026-11-15: "Cut me off there." was read as a stop, her answer was never spoken, and the lines after it went unheard). A stop only the model read into a line gives the speaker a fresh reply window (session), and it counts even when the model calls the line not for her, if it comes from the person she is talking with ("okay, that's enough, thanks"); every stop is logged.
- "Angel?" with the question still to come waits up to `followUpMs` (12 s) for the same speaker's next line.
- **A bare call while she holds or looks up is the start of a question** (0.1.3, the self-test of 2026-11-15: "Hey Angel, quick one." ran the check out of room and the plain rule called it a go-ahead). Her name with only words that announce a question ("quick one", "quick question", "got a sec", "I have a question", "one more thing") is ask, only the start, both in `HOLD_SYSTEM` and in the plain rule; anything more on the line keeps the old reading ("hold on Angel, quick question" is a wait). The check has 600 tokens of room like the gate (you pay only for what is written), is asked for the JSON alone on one line, and a reply cut off after a whole verdict is still used: the room was not raised further because a longer reply means the model wandered off the format, and a whole verdict before the cut is all it needs. A cut-off reply that holds a second "kind" (it changed its mind) is not used, and a used one is marked `cutOff`, so the log and the self-test report show it.
- **The "was that for me?" check gets the same guard** (`Test Angel.cmd`, 2026-11-16: "Hold on, I wasn't done." ran it past 600 tokens): a whole verdict before the cut is used and marked (the whole span first, then the first object with a real `to_angel`, as the hold check), one followed by a second `to_angel` is not. When a reply can't be used, both checks hand back how it began (`replyStart`, 160 characters) for the self-test report only, so the next reading can see why; the session's log keeps verdicts, never words. Weighed and left: a shorter reply format (the same four fields serve the session; the cut came from what followed the JSON, not the JSON).

- **A go-ahead can add to the question** (field test 2026-11-21: "...take a feat there if you... Now you can speak." was read as a go-ahead and the half she held was spoken): `HOLD_SYSTEM` has an `adds` flag, true for a go-ahead whose line also finishes or adds to the held question, with `question` written as for an ask; `parseHold` trusts only a real `true` on a go-ahead. The plain rule never sets it.

## How it works
- `mentionsAngel` and `isStopRequest` are regular expressions; plurals and "angelic" don't match, "Angel's" does. A stop is her name at the start (after an optional "hey", "okay", "so" or "um"), a stop word, and at most three words after it, in a line of at most 120 characters.
- `gate` sends the system prompt and "Earlier lines … LAST line …" (the line with its speaker, `lastText`) and expects `{"to_angel", "complete", "stop", "question"}`; `parseGate` reads it even inside a code fence. If the reply can't be read, or Claude can't be reached, the fallback is the plain rule on what was said (`bareText`, without the speaker's name): her name first, then a request. The session logs the failure.
- `holdCheck` and `readyLine` use the check's model (`gateModel`) and return their own cost; `parseHold` trusts only the five kinds (the whole `{...}` span first, then the first object alone, so words with braces after the JSON don't hide it), then the plain rule: stop, not for her, a bare call (`CALL_ONLY`, the whole rest of the line one of a fixed list, after her name and greetings are taken out), a hold-on, her name and little else, a question; `cleanReady` takes the quotes off and refuses more than 12 words or 90 characters. Both readers are in the timing test.
- Each call returns its own cost (`dollars`), worked out from that call's usage.

## Traps
- The fallback once read the speaker-prefixed line ("Mira: Angel, ...") and so never matched: every spoken question was lost whenever the check failed (review M1, 2026-11-13). Hence `bareText`, and a test with the prefix.
- The first stop pattern let "anything, then a stop word" match in many ways: a 60-word line took 1.3 s, and the whole bot (every speaker, the voice) waits while a regex runs (second review, 2026-11-13). It now matches only at the start and counts the words after with plain code, and the fallback reads only the first 300 characters. A new pattern gets a line in the timing test.
- The system prompt is too short for Haiku's prompt cache to take; the `cache_control` mark is harmless and costs nothing.

## Open items

Fails safe (not a stop, and the plain rule only needs her name). To weigh in a later build: reading the yes/no fields written before the cut, or a shorter reply format.
