## Decisions made
<!-- Rhea's answers, each with the question it answered and the date. -->
All asked one at a time in the design chat of 2026-11-15 (Opus), from her ask of 10:32 ("she cuts in with a quick, I'm ready style responce and then waits for a gap or somone to give some type of go ahead") and her standing rule from the same message (never scripted). Every pick was the recommended option; the design doc's d-7 has them with the options offered.
- When is the table busy? "Anyone talking (Recommended)": anyone, the asker included; blips under about half a second don't count.
- When does she cut in? "After a short wait (Recommended)": about 2 s for a natural gap first; only if they are still talking.
- Her quick reaction over a busy table? "Skip it (Recommended)": she cuts in at most once per question.
- What releases her after the cut-in? "A gap or a go-ahead (Recommended)": about 1.5 s of quiet, or anyone inviting her.
- How long does she hold it? "A minute, then let it go (Recommended)": no second cut-in; a note on the post; "what was it?" still works.
- A softer "hold on"? "Wait for an invite (Recommended)": then only a go-ahead releases her, or the minute runs out.
- A new question while she holds? "Asker: all in one; others: in turn (Recommended)".
- A merged question and another player's waiting one? "Merged keeps its place (Recommended)" (settles review 10's finding 6 from 0.1.2).
- The live round? "A player or friend (Recommended)": about 15 minutes with a second person in the channel.
Angel's choices within them (session.md and floor.md say why): who is talking comes from Discord's speaking signal (free); the first wait is for a pause of 0.8 s, the later gap 1.5 s; her "I'm ready" is written by one tiny Haiku call when a hold begins, voiced, never a stock line (if it can't be written she waits without it); a player who opted out still counts as talking, other bots don't; talk that began while she spoke counts from the end of her speech plus 0.5 s (her voice in a mic); the asker's reply window opens when she lets an answer go; another player's question releases the held answer and then waits for a gap of its own; the merged question after a hold may cut in again with fresh words; new settings `busyTable`, `busyBlipMs`, `busyPauseMs`, `busyFirstWaitMs`, `busyGapMs`, `busyMaxHoldMs`.

**Review 1** (an independent agent, 2026-11-15; reading list: the code diff, the new tests, this record, floor.md and session.md; it ran probes in a scratch folder). Ten findings, each checked against the code:
1. A "stop" verdict while she waits called the global stop, dropping another player's question queued behind the held one with no word to them, and ignored review 11's kept reply window. **True** (its probe and mine). Fixed: while the hold is live a stop drops only the held answer; after it, a stop with the reply window kept, as in the usual check. Test: Sam's waiting question is answered.
2. A merge while she waits left the held answer's post up (two written answers) and the merged answer waited for the held one to finish being written. **True.** Fixed: the held entry is dropped (`mergedAway`) even while it is still being written (its cost still counted), and its post is taken away once she lets it go. Test: one post left, the fresh answer spoken within 0.7 s while the held one takes 1.5 s.
3. The plain rule (when the check can't be reached) read "Hold on Angel." as a go-ahead and "Angel?" as a question. **True** (ran it). Fixed: hold words make a wait; her name with little else is a go-ahead. Test.
4. Her quick reaction coming back through a mic while she waits was checked (and "Hang on, pulling the page." could read as a wait). **True** by reading. Fixed: the echo check uses her reaction and her cut-in for this question. Test.
5. A speaker counted as a person at their start and not at their end (a member not yet cached) never got an end: busy for good. **True** by reading. Fixed: the voice link decides at the start and always passes that speaker's end on; and the floor takes a stretch still open after ten minutes as ended, so nothing can hold it for good. Tests.
6. A hold blocks the queue (the next answer isn't worked out until the hold ends), and a typed question wasn't a go-ahead. **True.** The typed question is fixed (a typed question from someone else releases her; test). The queue: kept as is and put under Open items in session.md: answers are spoken one at a time and the held answer goes first (Rhea's d-7); working the next one out during a hold needs the hold and the queue to be split, a bigger change for later if the live round shows the wait.
7. "Angel?" from someone else while she waits released her, but the question they said next had no name and was dropped. **True** by reading. Fixed: that call is kept as pending, as any "Angel?" is. Test.
8. Back-to-back answers: the next one's floor is judged while she is still speaking, and talk that began during her speech counts only after it. **True, by design** (her own voice in a mic would otherwise hold every answer); under Open items in session.md.
9. While her words were written and voiced she didn't watch for a pause or the time limit. **True.** Fixed: a pause or the limit during that time counts. Test.
10. Minor: (a) a `takeFloor` that throws left the answer counted as said: **true**, fixed (logged, she speaks); (b) a failed ready call's cost: **false** (a call that fails returns no usage to count); (c) the cut-in not in `lastSaid`: **true but harmless** (only a reply-window line is compared with it, and her answer's words replace it at once); left.
Each fix was checked by breaking it (all failed but three, whose tests were then tightened until they did); the busy, floor and voice tests ran 16 times side by side with no failure; the one-off lint found nothing new.
**Review 2** (a fresh independent agent on the code with review 1's fixes, 2026-11-15; it ran probes). Eight findings, each checked:
1. A quiet right after a line released her before that line was heard and judged (the speaking signal ends about 0.9 s before the piece of speech reaches her, then transcription and the check take a second or more, against a 1.5 s gap). So "Oh, and with a shield?", "Hold on Angel" or "Never mind" followed by quiet: she spoke anyway, and a late "never mind" called the global stop. **True** (its probe; I reasoned it through the code). Fixed: a pause or gap doesn't release her while a piece of speech said after the held question is still being heard or judged (`hearingNow`, `stillHearing`), and every pause or gap is at least `silenceMs` + 0.2 s, so that piece is already on its way when the quiet is judged. Tests: the three lines with slow hearing and a slow check; the gap's minimum.
2. After a merge, the deleted post was still given to the fresh answer as "your last written answer". **True** (its probe). Fixed: the previous one is put back. Test.
3. A merged-away answer's later sentences were still voiced (paid for, though not played) and set `lastSaid`. **True.** Fixed: nothing more of it is voiced. Test (no voice requested for them).
4. The let-go note could push a long post past Discord's 2,000 characters, losing the note (or the whole small-talk post). **True** (its probe). Fixed: `withNote` shortens the answer, never the note. Test.
5. A held typed question had no asker id, so the asker's own typed line released her. **True** (its probe). Fixed: a typed question knows its asker; their own typed line waits its turn (a typed addition isn't merged: merging is for spoken lines, as before). Test.

8. Tests that passed for the wrong reason (instant stand-ins, the table released only after the verdict). **True**; the new tests above use real-like delays.

**Review 3** (a fresh independent agent on review 2's fixes, 2026-11-15; it ran probes). Seven findings, each checked:
1. Two "the talk stopped, go ahead" shortcuts skipped the "still being heard" block: a "hold on" said as the talk stopped early in the hold was spoken over. **True** (its probe). Fixed: she goes ahead only when nobody is talking and nothing said is still being heard; talk that stopped with a line still being heard means no cut-in and a wait for it. Tests: the first wait, and the moment her words are ready.
2. A speaker talking since before the question (one piece that began before it) was never waited on or checked. **True** (its probe). Fixed: pieces and lines go by when they ended, so talk that ran on past the question counts (the asker's own earlier lines aside). Test.
3. A very short sound after the talk ("Wait!", under the blip length) was not waited for. **True** (its probe). Fixed: every wait also needs `silenceMs` + 0.2 s since any sound at all (`sinceAnyTalk`; a blip still isn't talk). Test.
4. A hung hearing or check held her the whole minute and let the answer go. **True**; the minute was still a sure exit. Fixed: a piece stuck over ten seconds stops holding her up. Test.
5. The note cut the page links (and could split an emoji), not the answer. **True** (its probe). Fixed: `typedAnswer` takes the note and makes room for it out of the answer, cut at a whole character; `withNote` removed. Test (an-15).
6. After a merge or a stop, `lastSaid` held the dropped answer's first sentence. **True.** Fixed: it is her reaction and her "I'm ready". Test.
7. A typed asker's spoken lines go the "someone else" way (a spoken "Angel?" isn't kept pending for them, a spoken addition isn't merged). **True, harmless**; left (merging is for spoken questions).
Each fix was checked by breaking it (one test stretched until it caught its break); 240 tests pass; the timing tests ran 16 times side by side with no failure; the lint is clean of new problems.
**Review 4** (a fresh independent agent on review 3's fixes, 2026-11-15; it ran probes against this and the previous commit). Six findings, each checked:
1. A "hold on" whose talk stopped longer ago than a breath (0.4 s) but whose piece hadn't come yet (it comes 0.9 s after the last sound) was spoken over at the end of a wait. **True** (its probe). 2. The "any sound at all" margin let a trickle of clicks block every gap, so she let every answer go. **True** (its probe; a regression from review 3). 3. The talk stopping as she would cut in and then starting again left her silent for the whole minute. **True** (its probe). 4. A piece's end worked out from its audio is early when the speaker paused inside it. **True** by reading. 5. The stuck-piece cap counted from arrival. **True, fails safe.** 6. `lastSaid` could become empty. **True, low.**
Three rounds of patches to the "is the quiet safe" rule had each opened the next hole, so it was **replaced by one rule** (`unheard`): a quiet counts only when nothing said since the held question is still unknown: no piece still being heard or judged, and no talk long enough to be words (`minUtteranceMs`, the shortest piece that is ever transcribed) whose piece hasn't reached her yet (a piece comes `silenceMs` after the last sound; it is waited for up to 1.5 s more). Shorter sounds are never transcribed, so they aren't waited for (2). A piece is dated by when it reached her (4). The stuck-piece cap is 30 s (listening has 20 s, the check 8 s and a retry) (5). The "any sound at all" margin is gone. If the talk starts again before she has cut in, she cuts in then, once (3); the time limit counts while her words are written. When nothing was said for a question, her last words are put back (6).
Tests: the 'hold on' case and the starting-again case with a live-like listening silence (0.8 s, longer than a breath, which a 0.3 s test silence could never show), a trickle of clicks, her last words kept; the session tests now send the piece a stopping speaker makes. One fresh set of 40 break-checks against the current code: every one failed its tests. 244 tests pass; the timing tests ran 16 times side by side with no failure.
